Warbell v1.0.0 is the first release of the new world: a living island with five biomes, working villages, realistic animals and people, and a much more readable fight.

## World

- The island is rebuilt around the castle with a new countryside: farmstead, croft, hedges, dry-stone walls, groves and levelled yards between the castle meadow and the biomes.
- Biome borders blend softly instead of meeting at hard seams. Snow, desert, rock, forest and swamp each keep their own ground, plants and weather.
- Five distinct ork camps replace the old single layout: a shaman ritual ground, caravanserai raiders, a tally-wall cave, a stilt cage deck and snow hunters.
- The desert rival stronghold is a fortified town with gatehouse, walls and its own producers.
- Story sites are scattered across the island: a sunken mission chapel, clan gong, signal tower, cut rope bridge, Old Ones' door, trading stones, a plundered caravan, a bell-casting pit and more.
- Distant isles rise out of the sea as real mountain terrain, and a waterfall pours from the rock massif into a winding valley.
- Roads route over the terrain instead of cutting straight through it, and bridges span real gaps.

## Villages and people

- Villagers follow a daily schedule: they work, eat, sit on benches, haggle at the market, chat and chase each other as children.
- Townsfolk have new proportions and models: adults with fuller builds, real child bodies, women in kirtles and coifs, and cloth in scanned linen, wool and leather.
- Villagers path around props, keep personal space and no longer sit inside market stalls.
- Settler huts, a merchant shop, thatched daub houses, meadow props (hay cart, scarecrow, skep, firewood rack) and a training pell fill out the town.
- Gate braziers, lanterns, a torsion ballista and a paddock fence finish the castle town.

## Animals and creatures

- Goat, dog, camel, wolf, brown bear, polar bear and deer are realistic skinned models with their own walking gaits.
- Ravens, rabbits and pike replace the older script-built creatures.
- Vultures soar, fold their wings to feed and glide on slow beats.

## Combat

- Sword swings are committed: no more snap-and-freeze, and the trail is smooth.
- Hits draw real blood, and each strike has layered impact sounds.
- Blood and steel sparks are sized to real scale.
- Heavy chops come out of a held charge stance.

## Cutscenes and story

- Chapter 2 opens with a dawn farm scene. The bell-in-the-tower cutscene is re-cut as a film with a letterboxed look, rack focus and handheld camera.
- Act I voice lines are recorded and cut, with a new Act I opening aboard the ship and a storm loop in the cabin.
- Village residents speak with their own voices, including new dialogue for the people of the market and the bench.
- Cutscene music is spotted to the scenes, and silence is kept where it belongs.

## Look and performance

- Water has rippled reflections, dark inland water and reflections of the sky.
- Light is less flat: a stronger sun key against the fill light and a warmer olive day grade.
- Far trees become impostors and undergrowth thins out gradually instead of popping.
- Props, biome statics and skinned crowds use automatic level-of-detail, cutting GPU load by roughly a fifth.
- Shadows from small static meshes and off-screen townsfolk are dropped past a distance, so distant scenes render faster.
- Footsteps respond to the surface you walk on: mud, sand, snow and shallow water each sound and leave different prints.

## Fixed

- New Game no longer keeps the previous run's castle house and wall collision.
- The boot-time render crash ("Binning main entity already binned") is fixed.
- Ground cover no longer stretches at the Extreme render distance.
- The first-person view looks out of the real third-person knight, which is now right-handed.
The Windows installer is self-signed. Windows SmartScreen may show **Unknown Publisher**; choose **More info → Run anyway** to install.
